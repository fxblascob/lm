PUBLICACIONES

"An Integration of services (voice, data, video and security) in the ULPnet network of the Univ. of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria.".

EUNIS'98. Congress on European Co-operation in Higher Education Information Systems.
Praga (Republica Checa). (Sep, 1998)
Dr. Enrique Rubio Royo (rubio@cicei.com)
Dr. Antonio Ocón Carreras (ocon@cicei.com)
Dr. Antonio González Guerra
Mario Marrero Ruiz

CICEI - Universidad de Las Palmas de Gran Canaria

Abstract

As one of the first stages of its Information Technologies Plan, the University of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria (Canary Islands, Spain) deployed during last year an ATM Asynchronous Transfer Mode) infrastructure for the corporate university network ULPnet, allowing the interconnection of 23 buildings along three campuses. Each of this campus is several kilometers away from the others, externally gathered via mono-mode fiber provided by Telefonica (Spanish PTT), and using multi-mode fiber inside.

The first services provided were the substitution of the existing legacy LANs using Ethernet Lanswitches (total 48) connected to the ATM backbone and providing more than 3,000 dedicated Ethernet connections logically arranged into several "virtual LANs" on behalf of LANE (LAN Emulation) services distributed into the 12 ATM switches. Every ATM link is at 155 Mbps, with some trunks migrating to 640 Mbps in the near future. The central servers are directly connected to the ATM switches (at 155 Mpbs) or via Fast Ethernet (100 Mbps). The second step was the integration of the voice PABXs into the ATM backbone by encapsulating the existing commercial E1 links into ATM cells. In fact, the economic impact of that measure practically has provided the funds necessary to accomplish more than one half of the whole project's budget.

The third (and perhaps more revolutionary) stage has been the integration of the security and surveillance system on the ATM backbone by using CODECs (coders-decoders) to transport real-time high-quality video from the surveillance cameras (and pan-tilt-zoom control signals) over ATM cells and an Ethernet virtual LAN to integrate an sophisticated access control system. Once again the revenues of the investment in ATM infrastructure has been large, allowing us to have more and better control with fewer labor hours. At this time we are developing the Multimedia ATM Network, integrating the video and real-time video-conferencing over LAN with video servers on-demand, trying to push one step beyond the investment made on ATM switches, LANswitches and CODECs.

First step - Migration from legacy LAN to ATM LanE

The original legacy Ethernet based-network, was spread over 10 buildings distributed on 3 physical locations (campuses), originally integrated via an FDDI (Fiber Distributed Data Interface) ring in the main campus and via 64/128 Kpbs serial links with the other campuses, being the standard protocols TCP/IP and IPX managed with 9 Cisco routers.

The ATM network has meant a great improvement, not only for the substantial speed of operation, but also in the topology and operational parameters of the network. The use of LANE (Lan Emulation) allowed the definition of VLANs (Virtual LANs) that permit the superation of the former topology-based networks. Thus, the new topology is not determined by the geographic dispersion of the equipment, but by the concept of "community of users" and by the practical size of such communities in terms of "broadcast noise". The experience we had about such a size is that such an community of users should not exceed 500 machines employing TCP/IP and IPX protocols, and for practical considerations of TCP/IP, class-C (254 nodes) were adopted. Until the new protocol MPOA (MultiProtocol Over ATM) can be operative in every component of the ULPnet, the actual approach has been the interconnection of the VLANs with two redundant Cisco 4700 routers. This solution has a drawback regarding a slight penalty in the speed of operation between different VLANs, not measured in terms of ATM speed, but instead of forwarding capacity of the routers. To avoid this problem, the main servers (around 20) of the network have been integrated in the ATM infrastructure and logically joined to all the VLANs (12 at this time).

Second step - Integration of voice over ATM

As noted in the previous section, an important part of the budget for the whole project became a consequence of integrating the existent PABX installation into the ATM infrastructure, by overriding the high cost of interconnection between these modules. This has been accomplished with special interface cards (developed by Fore and Northern Telecom) that encapsulates E1 (at 2 Mbps) links into streams of ATM cells. At this time, all of the links have been migrated to ATM successfully; the only experienced blackouts were due to the maintenance shutdowns of the switches. Obviously, every ATM equipment is provided with dual redundant power systems and UPS.

Third step - Security and Surveillance system over ATM

Without any doubt, an ATM infrastructure can be justified in terms of price/performance ratio, but this justification is void unless the performance becomes necessary. Our experience is that even "collapsing the backbone", by putting almost the network servers in the "neural center" of the network, the bandwidth demanded is far below the several 155 Mbps links available, as noted in the figure. This way, it becomes transparent the need to take full profit of the investment by integrating another services into the main network. One opportunity to do so appeared when the ULPGC staff was negotiating the "security contract" involving access control and surveillance systems. Instead the former approach of heterogeneous access control systems on different buildings and many labor hours of security personnel, we try to capitalize the investment in network infrastructure by means of the use of a powerful integrated access control system (as seen in figure 2) and reduce the labor hours by deploying surveillance cameras in different parts of the campuses (a total number of 18), centralizing both services in an "security center" in the main campus.

The access control system is based on APC-PSU (Intelligent Access Control and Monitoring Panels) units from Sensormatic, that integrates Card-Readers on 19 different rooms, controlled in a centralized-decentralized fashion with 1 central and 7 local servers using C-Cure 800 software running on Windows-NT machines, being the whole system operating under an dedicated VLAN of the ATM backbone. This approach has the advantages of the "local granularity" on the definition of access permissions whilst the concentration of surveillance persons 24 hours a day/365 days a year. The surveillance cameras integration has been accomplished with six Fore AVA-300 analog video encoders (distributed along the campuses) and three ATV-300 decoders on the central control location. The cameras are provided with PTZ (Pan/Tilt/Zoom) controls, whose signals are codified and decodified with the AVA and ATV equipment. Until now, both systems are behaving as expected, and the overall quality of service has experienced a significant growth, despite of the decrement of persons involved.

Fourth step - Integrated Multimedia Network

Once deployed the ATM network, our users are demanding more and more bandwidth by the growing utilization of multimedia on every kind of applications. At this time, our main effort is dedicated to the establishment of equipment (at the same rate as the protocols such as H-323 are becoming standards) that permits the real-time videoconferencing, both inside-inside and inside-outside towards IP-ISDN (H-323 to H-320) bridges. Also is in developing and initial testing the deployment of real-time video servers, which task is actually accomplished with Intel-based servers with Linux-based software.

Conclusions

In a few words, it can be said that the ATM network had more than satisfied our expectations. The major trade-on has been the consideration of the network as an sole entity, by reducing redundancies and dual expenses. On the other side, its major trade-off is also derived from the same principle: is necessary to consider the network as the most important resource, for which every active and proactive maintenance cost has to be approved.
